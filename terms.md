## What Thaw is, and isn't

Thaw plays a short guided breath before apps you choose, then lets you decide whether to open them. It's a gentle
pause, not a blocker: you can always continue to the app.

Thaw isn't a medical device and doesn't diagnose, treat or prevent any condition. If breathing exercises make you
dizzy or uncomfortable, stop, and breathe normally. Thaw can't tell whether you breathe along, and doesn't try.

Thaw works through Apple's Shortcuts app, which you set up and control. iOS, Shortcuts and the apps you pause are
made by others and can change; we'll do our best to keep Thaw working with them, but can't promise it always will.

## Free and Thaw Pro

- **Free:** the pause before 3 apps (the first 3 different apps your shortcut hands Thaw), and the breath on its
  own, any time.
- **Thaw Pro** pauses more than 3 apps. It's sold in Thaw through Apple's App Store as:
  - **Thaw Pro Monthly:** an auto-renewing subscription, 1 month, US$2.99 a month.
  - **Thaw Pro Yearly:** an auto-renewing subscription, 1 year, US$14.99 a year. New subscribers may get the first
    week free (Apple decides who's eligible; Thaw shows it only when you are).
  - **Thaw Pro Lifetime:** a single purchase, US$39.99, with nothing to renew.

  Prices are shown in your own currency before you buy, and may differ by country.

### Auto-renewing subscriptions

- Payment is charged to your Apple Account when you confirm the purchase, or when a free week ends.
- A subscription renews automatically for the same length and price unless you cancel it at least 24 hours before
  the end of the current period (or of the free week). Your Apple Account is charged for the renewal within the 24
  hours before the period ends.
- Manage or cancel in Thaw (Settings → Manage Subscription) or on your iPhone (Settings → your name →
  Subscriptions). After cancelling, Pro lasts until the end of the period you've paid for.
- If you buy a subscription during a free week, any unused part of the free week ends.
- If a price ever goes up, Apple tells you first and, where the law or Apple requires it, asks you to agree.
